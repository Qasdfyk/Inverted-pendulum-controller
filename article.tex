    \section{Modeling of inverted pendulum on cart system}
	\label{sec:pp}
    \hl{Mam prosbe aby wstawil Pan tu rys. 2.1 z pracy, opis ponizej bedzie nawiazywal do rysunku. Chyba z tego co pamietam to Pan go rysowal. Wyglada ladnie. The inverted pendulum on a cart is a well-known underactuated mechanical system and a widely
used benchmark for testing control algorithms. It consists of a
cart of mass $M$ that moves along a horizontal track, with a pendulum of mass $m$ and length
$l$ attached to it by a frictionless pivot. The only control input is a horizontal force $u(t)$
applied to the cart; an external disturbance $F_\mathrm{w}(t)$ acting horizontally on the
pendulum tip is also considered. The physical parameters used in this work are listed in
Table~\ref{tab:params}.

The following simplifying assumptions are made: the rod is massless and
rigid; friction at the wheels and at the pivot is negligible; motion is planar; and the
pendulum mass is treated as a point mass at the rod tip. The angle $\theta(t)$ measures the
pendulum deflection from the upright position (positive clockwise, $\theta=0$ at the top),
and $x(t)$ is the cart position.

\subsection{Equations of motion}
\hl{dopisac (t) w rozdz. 2.1, 2.2}
The position of the pendulum tip in the fixed frame is
$x_G = x + l\sin\theta$, $y_G = l\cos\theta$.
Differentiating twice and applying Newton's second law to the whole system in the horizontal
direction gives:
\begin{equation}
(M{+}m)\ddot{x} + ml\cos\theta\,\ddot{\theta} - ml\sin\theta\,\dot{\theta}^{2} = u + F_\mathrm{w}.
\label{eq:trans}
\end{equation}
Taking moments about the pivot:
\begin{equation}
ml\cos\theta\,\ddot{x} + ml^{2}\ddot{\theta} = mgl\sin\theta + lF_\mathrm{w}\cos\theta.
\label{eq:rot}
\end{equation}
Introducing $M_T = M+m$ and $D(\theta)=l\bigl(M_T - m\cos^{2}\!\theta\bigr)$, solving
\eqref{eq:trans}--\eqref{eq:rot} for the accelerations gives:
\begin{align}
\ddot{x} &= \frac{u + ml\sin\theta\,\dot{\theta}^{2} - mg\sin\theta\cos\theta}{M_T - m\cos^{2}\!\theta},
\label{eq:xdd}\\[2pt]
\ddot{\theta} &= \frac{M_T g\sin\theta - \cos\theta\!\left(u + ml\sin\theta\,\dot{\theta}^{2}\right)}{D(\theta)}.
\label{eq:tdd}
\end{align}

\subsection{Nonlinear state-space model}

Defining the state vector $x(t) = [\theta,\dot{\theta},x,\dot{x}]^T$,
the dynamics \eqref{eq:xdd}--\eqref{eq:tdd} take the form $\dot{x} = f(x,u,F_\mathrm{w})$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{bmatrix}\dot{x}_1\\\dot{x}_2\\\dot{x}_3\\\dot{x}_4\end{bmatrix}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
x_2\\[2pt]
\dfrac{M_T g\sin x_1 - \cos x_1\!\left(u + ml\sin x_1\,x_2^{2}\right)}{D(x_1)}\\[6pt]
x_4\\[2pt]
\dfrac{u + ml\sin x_1\,x_2^{2} - mg\sin x_1\cos x_1}{M_T - m\cos^{2}\!x_1}
\end{bmatrix}.
\label{eq:nonlin_ss}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Linearized model}
\hl{rownania w calej pracy co sa w tekscie przeniesc jak rownania - equations, czy align i wyjasnic symbole uzyte, A, B, Bd trzeba wyjasnic, trzeba podac wymiarowosc macierzy wektorow, zdefiniowac najpierw liczbe zmiennych n, m, r w modelu przestrzenie stanu i to pomoze przy opisie}
For small deflections around the upright equilibrium ($\theta\approx 0$), the approximations
$\sin\theta\approx\theta$, $\cos\theta\approx 1$ yield the linear time-invariant
model $\dot{x} = Ax + Bu + B_\mathrm{d}F_\mathrm{w}$:
\begin{equation}
A = \begin{bmatrix}0&1&0&0\\ \dfrac{M_T g}{Ml}&0&0&0\\[4pt]0&0&0&1\\[2pt]-\dfrac{mg}{M}&0&0&0\end{bmatrix},\;
B = \begin{bmatrix}0\\[2pt]-\dfrac{1}{Ml}\\[4pt]0\\[2pt]\dfrac{1}{M}\end{bmatrix}.
\label{eq:linear}
\end{equation}
The eigenvalues of $A$ are $\lambda_{1,2}=0$ and $\lambda_{3,4}\approx\pm 5.49$; the positive
eigenvalue confirms that the system is open-loop unstable. The pair $(A,B)$ is fully
controllable (rank~4), so a stabilising feedback law exists.

    \section{Control system design}
	\label{sec:r}
    The shared state vector is
$x(k)=[\theta(k),\dot{\theta}(k),x(k),\dot{x}(k)]^T$, where the components are the pendulum
angle, angular velocity, cart position, and cart velocity, respectively. The control signal
$u(k)$ is the force on the cart at time $t = kT_p$.
All controllers work in discrete time with sampling period $T_p$. 

\subsection{PID control scheme}
\hl{Prosze tymczasowo dodac cytowanie do kazdego algorytmu jezeli w publikacji cytowanej byly dokladnie te same rownania algorytmu przedstawione np. "The parallel PID structure [1]..." - uzyc cite oczywiscie. Jezeli byly roznice w rownaniach to nie cytowac. Uzyc references.bib}
The parallel PID structure uses two independent PID loops whose outputs are added together:
\begin{equation}
u(k) = u_{\theta}(k) + u_{x}(k),
\end{equation}
where $e_{\theta}(k)=\theta_\mathrm{ref}-x_1(k)$ and $e_x(k)=x_\mathrm{ref}-x_3(k)$.
\hl{Powyzsze przesunac i zrobic jako rownania i wyjsnic pod (8) a potem pod (9) i prosze po pokazaniu kazdego wzoru, zdefiniowac wszystkie symbole co nie byly zdefiniowane: Kp itd.}
Each loop uses a discrete-time PID law with \emph{derivative on measurement}, which avoids
amplifying measurement noise:
\begin{align}
u_{\theta}(k) &= K_{p,\theta}e_{\theta}(k) + K_{i,\theta}\!\sum_{j=0}^{k}\!e_{\theta}(j)T_p
               - K_{d,\theta}x_2(k),\label{eq:pid_theta}\\
u_{x}(k) &= K_{p,x}e_x(k) + K_{i,x}\!\sum_{j=0}^{k}\!e_x(j)T_p
           - K_{d,x}x_4(k).\label{eq:pid_x}
\end{align}
Using the velocity states $x_2$ and $x_4$ as derivative feedback directly removes the
derivative kick that would otherwise occur during reference steps. \hl{Prosze przedstawic antiwindup - rownania i potem bedzie komentarz}An anti-windup mechanism
clamps the integral to $\pm 5.0$.

\subsection{LQR control scheme}
\hl{wyjasnic nie zdefiniowane wczesniej symbole (pojawiajace sie po prawej stronie rownanania) od razu pod rownaniem jak sie wprowadza nowy symbol, przesledzic, nie wszedzie sa}
The Discrete Linear Quadratic Regulator (DLQR) computes a
state-feedback gain $K$ that minimises the infinite-horizon cost:
\begin{equation}
J = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\!\left(x(k)^T Q\,x(k) + u(k)^T R\,u(k)\right),
\label{eq:lqr_cost}
\end{equation}
subject to the linearised discrete model $x(k{+}1)=A_dx(k)+B_du(k)$,
where $A_d,B_d$ are obtained from $(A,B)$ of \eqref{eq:linear} via zero-order-hold (ZOH)
discretisation at period $T_p$. The optimal gain is found from the positive-definite solution $P$ of the Discrete Algebraic
Riccati Equation (DARE):
\begin{equation}
P = A_d^T P A_d - A_d^T P B_d(R+B_d^T P B_d)^{-1}B_d^T P A_d + Q,
\end{equation}
yielding:
\begin{equation}
K = (R + B_d^T P B_d)^{-1}B_d^T P A_d,
\end{equation}
and the control law $u(k) = -K\bigl(x(k) - x_\mathrm{ref}\bigr)$,
where $Q\succeq 0$, $R>0$ are design weighting matrices.

\subsection{LQR-I control scheme}

To eliminate steady-state cart-position error, the DLQR is augmented with an integrator
acting on the position tracking error. The accumulated error state is:
\begin{equation}
z(k+1) = z(k) + \bigl(x_\mathrm{ref} - x_3(k)\bigr)T_p.
\end{equation}
The augmented state $x_\mathrm{aug}(k)=[x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4,z]^T$ obeys:
\begin{equation}
x_\mathrm{aug}(k{+}1) = \tilde{A}_d\,x_\mathrm{aug}(k) + \tilde{B}_d\,u(k),
\end{equation}
where $\tilde{A}_d\in\mathbb{R}^{5\times 5}$ and $\tilde{B}_d\in\mathbb{R}^{5\times 1}$
append the integrator row to $A_d$ and $B_d$, respectively. Applying DLQR to the augmented
system with extended weighting matrices $\tilde{Q}$ and $R$ yields:
\begin{equation}
u(k) = -\tilde{K}\,x_\mathrm{aug}(k),
\end{equation}
which gives zero steady-state position error for constant setpoints.

\subsection{PID-LQR control scheme}

The PID-LQR scheme combines DLQR stabilisation with an outer PID loop on the position
error:
\begin{equation}
u(k) = u_\mathrm{LQR}(k) + u_\mathrm{PID,pos}(k),
\end{equation}
where the LQR component acts on the full state:
\begin{equation}
u_\mathrm{LQR}(k) = -K\bigl(x(k) - x_\mathrm{ref}\bigr),
\end{equation}
and $u_\mathrm{PID,pos}(k)$ follows the discrete PID law of \eqref{eq:pid_x} acting only on the
cart-position loop. This structure improves setpoint tracking and reduces steady-state errors
caused by model mismatch.

\subsection{Fuzzy LQR control scheme}

In Fuzzy-LQR, a Takagi-Sugeno (T-S) fuzzy block runs in parallel with the DLQR:
\begin{equation}
u(k) = u_\mathrm{LQR}(k) + G\cdot u_\mathrm{Fuzzy}(k).
\end{equation}
Triangular membership functions over two linguistic sets (\emph{small error},
\emph{large error}) for each of the four state variables yield a rule base of
$2^4=16$ rules of the form:
\begin{quote}
\textbf{IF} $e_\theta$ is $A_i$ \textbf{AND} $\dot\theta$ is $B_i$
$\ldots$ \textbf{THEN} $u_i = -K^{(i)}x$.
\end{quote}
The local gain vectors adapt to the severity of the deviation:
\begin{equation}
K^{(i)} = K_\mathrm{base} + \alpha\,L_i,
\end{equation}
where $L_i\in\{0,\ldots,4\}$ counts how many state variables are currently in the
\emph{large-error} region, and $\alpha$ is a fixed step. The defuzzified output is the
weighted average:
\begin{equation}
u_\mathrm{Fuzzy}(k) = \frac{\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{16}w_i(x)\,u_i}
                           {\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{16}w_i(x)},
\end{equation}
where $w_i$ is the firing strength of rule $i$ and $G$ is a global scaling gain. As a result,
the controller reacts more strongly at large deflections and more gently near the equilibrium.

\subsection{MPC-NO control scheme}

At each sampling instant the Nonlinear MPC (NMPC) solves the following finite-horizon
optimisation problem:
\begin{equation}
\min_{\Delta u(k)}\;J(k) = \sum_{p=1}^{N}e_p^T Q\,e_p
                              + \sum_{p=0}^{N_u-1}\Delta u_p^T R\,\Delta u_p,
\label{eq:mpc_cost}
\end{equation}

Do zdefiniowania wymiarowosci macierzy i wektorow uzywamy odpowiednich symboli juz zdefiniowanych. W rownaniu wyzej (21) nie jestem pewien czy rozumiem, bo nizej jest rownanie skalar a wyzej jest transpozycja, sugeruje wektor, a mamy znak sumy, prosze przesledzic, jak sie pokaze wszystko to bedzie jasne. Uwage zastosowac do wsystkich algorytmow, przesledzic, sprawdzic, poprawic jak trzeba.}
where $e_p(k)=x_\mathrm{ref}(k{+}p|k)-\hat{x}(k{+}p|k)$,
$\Delta u_p = u(k{+}p|k)-u(k{+}p{-}1|k)$, $N$ is the prediction horizon and
$N_u\leq N$ the control horizon, subject to:
\begin{align}
\hat{x}_{k+1} &= f\!\left(\hat{x}_k,u_k\right),\quad k=0,\ldots,N{-}1,\\
u_{\min} &\leq u_k \leq u_{\max}.
\end{align}
\hl{np indeksy czasowe w nawiasach (k), nie w subscript, przesledzic wszedzie aby bylo spojnie}
The prediction model $f(\cdot)$ propagates the nonlinear dynamics \eqref{eq:nonlin_ss} over
one sampling period using fourth-order Runge-Kutta (RK4), assuming the input is held constant
within each interval (ZOH). The optimisation is solved by the SLSQP algorithm from
\texttt{scipy.optimize}.

\subsection{MPC-NO-alt control scheme}

MPC-NO-alt extends the cost \eqref{eq:mpc_cost} by adding a penalty on the control amplitude,
which helps reduce actuator wear and energy use:
\begin{equation}
J(k) = \sum_{p=1}^{N}e_p^T Q\,e_p
      + \sum_{p=0}^{N_u-1}\!\left(\Delta u_p^T R\,\Delta u_p + u_p^T R_\mathrm{abs}\,u_p\right),
\end{equation}
where $R_\mathrm{abs}>0$ weights the force magnitude directly. The prediction model,
constraints, and solver are the same as in MPC-NO.

\subsection{LMPC control scheme}

In LMPC, the nonlinear RK4 prediction model is replaced with the ZOH-discretised linear model
from \eqref{eq:linear}:
\begin{equation}
\hat{x}_{k+1} = A_d\hat{x}_k + B_du_k,\quad k=0,\ldots,N{-}1,
\end{equation}
so that prediction reduces to simple matrix multiplications. The cost function is the same as
\eqref{eq:mpc_cost} and the SLSQP solver is reused. While the linear model loses accuracy at
large deflections, it is much faster to compute than NMPC.

\section{Control quality indicators}
\hl{Propnuje na razie wypunktowac: nazwa i wzor, a pod nim komentarz a nie przed wzorem i wyjasnienie symboli i zaznaczam, ze nie mozemy miec zdublowanych znaczen uzytych symboli w calym artykule np. jesli N to horyzont predykcji (wczesniej) to nie mozemy uzyc juz N jako liczba probek ale np. The following performance indices are used to compare all controllers. They are computed over
$N$ discrete samples of the output $y(k)$, which is either the pendulum angle $\theta$ or the
cart position $x$:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Mean Squared Error} (MSE) --- gives higher weight to large errors:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{MSE} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\bigl(y(k)-y_\mathrm{ref}(k)\bigr)^2.
\end{equation}
\item \textbf{Mean Absolute Error} (MAE) --- average absolute deviation:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{MAE} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\bigl|y(k)-y_\mathrm{ref}(k)\bigr|.
\end{equation}
\item \textbf{Integral of Squared Error} (ISE) --- cumulative quadratic error energy:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{ISE} = \sum_{k=1}^{N}\bigl(y(k)-y_\mathrm{ref}(k)\bigr)^2.
\end{equation}
\item \textbf{Integral of Absolute Error} (IAE) --- cumulative absolute error:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{IAE} = \sum_{k=1}^{N}\bigl|y(k)-y_\mathrm{ref}(k)\bigr|.
\end{equation}
\item \textbf{Quadratic control cost} $E_{u,L_2}$ and \textbf{absolute control cost}
$E_{u,L_1}$, measuring actuator effort:
\begin{align}
E_{u,L_2} &= \sum_{k=1}^{N}u(k)^{2},\\
E_{u,L_1} &= \sum_{k=1}^{N}|u(k)|.
\end{align}
\item \textbf{Settling time} $T_s$ --- the time after which $|\theta(k)|<\varepsilon_\theta =
0.001$~rad and $|x(k)|<\varepsilon_x = 0.002$~m remain satisfied (2\,\% of the initial
deflection).
\item \textbf{Overshoot} $M_p$ --- maximum percentage overshoot of $y$ above $y_\mathrm{ref}$
relative to the initial step size.
\item \textbf{Steady-state error} $e_{ss}$ --- mean error in the last 10\,\% of the simulation,
showing how well each controller holds the setpoint.
\end{itemize}
